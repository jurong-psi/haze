    What the numbers mean

    • 24-hour PSI decides green or red. It is NEA's official air-quality index and the measure Singapore's Ministry of Manpower points employers to. It is a rolling average: every hour the oldest hour drops out and the newest comes in, so it is published hourly but moves slowly and steadily.
    • 1-hour PM2.5 is a different measure. It is the amount of fine smoke particles in the air over the last hour, in µg/m³. It reacts first, but swings a lot during the day (highest in mid-afternoon), so it is shown as an early sign only.
    • The two are on different scales. A PM2.5 reading of 200 µg/m³ is not a PSI of 200, so the PM2.5 number cannot be compared with the 200 line.
    • They measure the same smoke. NEA scores six pollutants and the PSI is the highest score; during this haze that is always PM2.5. So the PSI is, in effect, the past 24 hours of PM2.5 turned into an index.
